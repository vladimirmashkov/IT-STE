# W3 — ITIL service-management terminology

State: in-progress

Question and scope: Which ITIL/service-management terms should be covered by the IT-STE documentation standard, and which existing ASD-derived rows need a new IT sense or part of speech? This slice covers GOV service management and its direct relations to support, change, configuration, asset, service-level, availability, resilience, and improvement language. It does not define API, protocol, cryptographic, software-runtime, UI, or physical meanings. The table below is a draft research inventory, not a canonical vocabulary or an approval list.

Alternatives and decision: the categories `new-term`, `new-sense`, `new-pos`, and `split proposal` below are research classifications only. They are not `it_disposition` values and do not approve any term.

| Candidate | Research classification | Bounded draft sense or boundary | Decision in this slice |
|---|---|---|---|
| `service` | `new-pos` + split proposal | A managed IT capability that provides a stated outcome to a user or other consumer. | Keep separate from ASD `service (v)` maintenance. Do not replace the existing verb or silently broaden it. |
| `alert` | `new-pos` + split proposal | A monitoring signal that indicates a condition requiring attention. | Keep separate from the ASD notification verb. Do not use it as a synonym for every event or incident. |
| `problem` | `new-sense` + split proposal | A cause or potential cause of one or more service incidents that requires investigation or control. | Materialize restricted draft `ITSTE-T-0003`; keep it separate from the existing general-difficulty noun, symptoms, incidents, and workarounds. |
| `incident` | `new-sense` + split proposal | An unplanned interruption to a managed service or a reduction in its quality. | Materialize restricted draft `ITSTE-T-0004`; keep it separate from the problem cause, event/alert signal, and security-specific incident. The formal glossary wording remains a human-review question. |
| `release` | `new-pos` + split proposal | A version or set of changed components made available for use. | Keep separate from the existing verb for freeing a lock/resource. Distinguish `release` from placing it into a target environment (`deployment`). |
| `known error` | `new-term` | A service-management problem that has been analyzed and for which a cause or workaround is recorded. | Materialize restricted draft `ITSTE-T-0005` as a phrase; keep it distinct from an unresolved problem, an incident, and the workaround. The exact formal glossary wording remains to be checked. |
| `workaround` | `new-term` | A temporary method that reduces the effect of an incident or problem without removing its underlying cause. | Materialize restricted draft `ITSTE-T-0006` as a noun; do not describe it as a permanent or root-cause fix. |
| `service request` | `new-term` | A user request for information, access, or a predefined service action. | Candidate only; requires a dedicated Service Request Management source before canonical admission. |
| `service desk` | `new-term` | A support function that receives, coordinates, and communicates service requests and incidents. | Candidate only; do not equate it automatically with a team, ticket queue, or escalation authority. |
| `configuration item` | `new-term` | A component controlled to deliver or support a service, with recorded attributes and relationships. | Strong candidate for a GOV/REL inventory entry; verify the formal scope against the detailed practice material. |
| `IT asset` | `new-term` | An item of value that an organization manages to deliver or support IT services. | Candidate only; asset ownership, financial lifecycle, and configuration control must not be conflated. |
| `service level agreement` | `new-term` | A documented agreement that states service commitments, responsibilities, and measurement conditions. | Candidate only; needs a dedicated Service Level Management source and a boundary from a target/indicator. |
| `service level target` | `new-term` | A measurable target for a service level under stated conditions and a defined period. | Candidate only; compare with `SLO` in the reliability slice rather than treating the terms as universal synonyms. |
| `availability` | `new-term` in the current inventory | The ability of a service or component to perform its agreed function when required. | Candidate only; requires an availability/continuity source and an observable measurement boundary. |
| `resilience` | `new-term` | The ability of a service or system to continue and recover within specified limits after disruption. | Candidate only; keep it distinct from availability and from an unqualified claim that a system is healthy. |
| `continual improvement` | `new-term` | An ongoing, measured activity to improve a service, practice, product, or outcome. | Practice-name candidate supported by the ITIL Version 5 page; exact wording and scope remain open. |
| `change authority` | `new-term` | A person or group authorized to assess, approve, schedule, or reject a change. | Park for the change-management slice; the inspected sources list Change Enablement but do not establish this definition. |
| `deployment` | `new-term` | The act of placing a release or change into a target environment. | Park for the change/release/deployment slice; keep it separate from the release artifact and from an approval decision. |

Unresolved candidates intentionally parked outside this slice are `escalation`, `urgency`, `service owner`, `standard change`, `emergency change`, `change schedule`, and `continuity`. They require their own source-backed wording and are not admitted or dispositioned by this inventory.

Useful controlled substitutions and exclusions are explicit: use `maintain` or `perform the specified maintenance` for the existing physical `service` verb; use `notify` or `send an alert` when the operation is delivery of a notification; use `reported failure` or `observed difficulty` when the general `problem` sense is intended; use `security incident` when the security process is intended; and name whether `release`, `deployment`, `change`, or lock/resource release is meant. Do not use ITIL to define protocol, cryptographic, software-runtime, UI, or physical meanings outside the service-management frame.

Fact versus inference: the ASD headwords, POS, meanings, page coordinates, and visual row findings are source facts. PeopleCert's practice names and public-page descriptions are external source facts. The proposed senses, split proposals, substitutions, and admission categories are editorial draft inferences for the IT-STE domain matrix. They are not official ASD or ITIL guidance, approval, release, publication, canonicalization, or SFT authorization.

Remaining uncertainty / escalation: PeopleCert's public pages expose practice overviews but not the complete detailed ITIL glossary/practice guides; the exact formal glossary wording for `incident`, `known error`, and `workaround` remains open and requires human review. `service request`, `service desk`, `IT asset`, `service level agreement`, `service level target`, `availability`, `resilience`, `continual improvement`, `change authority`, and `deployment` remain unresolved candidates. The term contract supports original IT-only draft records with external ITIL references, but broader source/version policy, term relations, and multi-sense review still require editorial review. No term is approved, released, or ready for SFT.
