Alternatives and decision: Resolve both blockers as `restricted` draft senses. For `APPROVED`, use `Permitted by an identified authority for a stated system, object, or use`; require the authority and scope in security/procedural prose. Reject `allowed` because an unqualified approval claim can omit who granted it and what it covers; reject `prohibited` because the term is useful when the approval record and scope are explicit. For `APPROXIMATE`, use `Close to a measured or calculated value; not exact and not a guaranteed limit`; use `estimated` for inferred values and `rounded value` only when precision was intentionally reduced. Reject unrestricted `allowed` because approximate values are unsuitable as hard security or capacity boundaries; retain `restricted` because the term is useful for monitoring and planning. These are draft editorial inferences: PDF and external-source findings are facts within their scopes, while the restrictions and examples are proposed IT applications.
