# W1 — D-P337-003 / page 337 / precision and accuracy
State: resolved-draft
Question and scope: Can `PRECISION` support a bounded IT measurement sense without being treated as accuracy? Scope is limited to the measurement property in the ASD row and does not define generic quality, numerical resolution, or vocabulary approval.

External evidence:
- [VIM3 2.15 measurement precision](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.15.html), Joint Committee for Guides in Metrology, VIM3 online entry, accessed 2026-10-23. It defines measurement precision as closeness of agreement between indications or measured values from replicate measurements under specified conditions; notes include repeatability, intermediate precision, and reproducibility, and explicitly state that using precision to mean accuracy is erroneous. This is directly controlling metrology terminology; it does not prescribe the IT-STE project’s editorial disposition.
- [Guidelines for Evaluating and Expressing the Uncertainty of NIST Measurement Results](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297), Barry N. Taylor and Chris E. Kuyatt, NIST Technical Note 1297, 1994 edition, web version accessed 2026-10-23. The inspected official NIST page identifies the guidance as a method for evaluating and expressing measurement uncertainty. It corroborates the metrology context, but the page-level content inspected here does not supply the controlling precision definition; VIM is the primary basis for the wording.
Alternatives and decision: Change the draft disposition to `restricted`. Define `precision` as “The closeness of agreement between repeated measurements under stated conditions; distinct from accuracy.” Permit it when the repeatability, intermediate-precision, or reproducibility conditions are stated or otherwise identifiable. Retain `repeatability under stated conditions` as a contextual alternative. Do not use `precision` to claim closeness to a reference value (accuracy), and do not use the term as an unrestricted synonym for numeric resolution. The ASD row and PDF findings are source facts; the bounded measurement restriction and examples are editorial inferences supported by VIM.
Remaining uncertainty / escalation: The ASD entry’s “accurate” wording is broader and less precise than VIM terminology. Editorial review should decide whether the project wants this bounded metrology sense or a different IT-specific usage; no owner decision is required for this draft restriction. No approval, canonicalization, release, or SFT readiness is claimed.
