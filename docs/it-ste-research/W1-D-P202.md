Question and scope: Does the ASD pair `CONTINUOUS` (adj) / `CONTINUOUSLY` (adv) support a bounded IT monitoring and execution sense? Scope is limited to an activity or condition continuing during a stated interval; it does not define sampling frequency, service availability, continuous delivery, or a general promise that periodic monitoring has no gaps.

NIST describes a continuous monitoring strategy/program as providing ongoing visibility and awareness. This is authoritative security-monitoring usage, but it does not establish that every observation or sample is uninterrupted.

Prometheus says an alert with a `for` duration must continue to be active during each evaluation for that duration before firing. This is primary implementation documentation for a bounded alert-condition sense; it does not define all uses of `continuous` or `continuously`.
Alternatives and decision: Change both records from `needs-review` to `allowed` while retaining `draft` / `editorial-review-needed`. Use `CONTINUOUS` = “Continuing without interruption during a stated interval, provisionally” and `CONTINUOUSLY` = “In a manner that continues without interruption during a stated interval, provisionally.” These definitions are editorial inferences constrained by the ASD glosses and the inspected IT evidence, not facts asserted by ASD, NIST, or Prometheus. Keep the interval-bounded examples. Reject “ongoing monitoring” as the whole definition because it is too broad and can include periodic observations; reject treating `continuous` as a guarantee of uninterrupted sampling without an explicit interval/condition. The visual row decision is source fact; the disposition and IT wording are draft editorial decisions.
Remaining uncertainty / escalation: NIST’s program-level use and Prometheus’s evaluation model do not settle every product’s meaning of continuous monitoring. Editorial review should confirm that this bounded sense is wanted for IT-STE; it does not establish vocabulary approval, release, or SFT readiness.
