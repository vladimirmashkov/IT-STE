This is specialist maintenance/safety evidence for observable physical wear and the need for inspection or disposition; it is aviation-specific and is not generalized into a universal IT maintenance rule.

Alternatives and decision: Change both records to `restricted`. Define `chafed` as physically worn or damaged by rubbing, especially cable or wire covering; retain `worn by rubbing` and `damaged insulation` as contextual alternatives. Use an explicit inspection, isolation, or replacement procedure and do not infer safe continued use from the adjective alone. Define `by chance` as an accidental or coincidental occurrence without evidence of intention or a specified causal mechanism. Retain `accidentally` for an unintended action and `coincidentally` for simultaneous occurrence without established causation; use `risk` for potential harm and likelihood. Reject unrestricted `allowed` because both terms can conceal a safety-relevant physical condition or unsupported troubleshooting conclusion. Reject `prohibited` because precise factual descriptions of wear and coincidence remain legitimate. The dispositions, definitions, examples, and safety boundaries are draft editorial inferences; the ASD rows and external sources do not approve IT vocabulary.
