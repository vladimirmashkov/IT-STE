## ITSTE-R-0001

### Admit vocabulary through documented IT meanings

Use general words from the applicable reviewed English vocabulary and domain terms admitted for the intended IT context\. Before admitting a general word\, record its definition\, part of speech\, permitted forms\, and usage restrictions\. Before admitting a technical noun or verb\, also identify the domain concept or operation\, its terminology authority\, and the product or specification version when relevant\. A noun position\, a familiar spelling\, or use in source code is not admission evidence\. Prefer an admitted general expression when it conveys the same operation and distinctions clearly\; admit a specialized term when it names a necessary concept or operation more accurately\. Submit missing or disputed entries to the responsible lexical editor with the proposed meaning\, authority\, context\, and an example\. Until review resolves the entry\, identify the unresolved terminology and do not claim lexical conformance\.

Applies to: general

### Exceptions

- The pilot contains no controlled vocabulary\. Hypothetical review decisions in these examples illustrate admission evidence\, not actual entries or global word approval\.

- Authoritative IT standards\, schemas\, product specifications\, and maintained project glossaries can support domain admission\. Do not inherit the ASD dictionary\, its technical\-term categories\, or its example vocabulary as an IT admission list\.

- An exact command\, identifier\, interface string\, or quotation can be preserved as a literal without admitting its words into editable prose\. Explain an unfamiliar literal outside its boundary\.

- Admission does not permit every use of a spelling\: review meaning\, grammatical function\, forms\, and context independently\. Do not label an ordinary unsupported use a technical term merely to bypass review\.

### Positive examples

Hypothetical admission record\: lease is a noun for a time\-limited right to hold a worker slot\, restricted to the scheduler specification\. The lexical editor records that definition\, its plural form\, the specification version\, and the scheduler domain before accepting the entry\.

Rationale: A concept\, authority\, grammatical function\, forms\, and scope make the proposed noun reviewable\; this is not an actual vocabulary admission\.

Hypothetical admission record\: drain is a verb meaning stop new work and wait for assigned work to finish\, under the worker API specification\. Submit that operation and its permitted forms for lexical review instead of replacing it with stop\.

Rationale: The technical operation differs from an immediate stop\. Domain admission can preserve that distinction instead of imposing an inaccurate general synonym\.

### Negative examples

The developer used frobnicate in a code comment\, so frobnicate is approved for every administration guide\.

Rationale: Code occurrence provides neither a reviewed meaning nor an authority or scope\. Submit the proposed term for review rather than infer general approval\.

Call any unfamiliar word a technical noun if it follows the article the\.

Rationale: A grammatical position is not evidence of a domain concept or admission\. General vocabulary and technical terms both need documented review\.

## ITSTE-R-0002

### Keep each term within its approved use

Before you use a term in editable prose\, check the applicable English lexical entry against the intended technical meaning and context\. Use only the meaning\, part of speech\, inflected form\, and usage context that the entry permits\. Check the entry\'s definition and restrictions\, not only its headword\. Approval of a spelling as a noun does not approve its use as a verb\, and approval as a verb does not approve its use as a noun\. Establish each function and meaning independently\, even when both uses are common in IT\. Use the permitted verb forms and adjective forms recorded or explicitly licensed by the applicable entry\; do not infer permission for a form from ordinary English usage alone\. Check suspected cross\-language false friends against the English definition and the technical context\. In an optional translation\, preserve the authoritative English meaning rather than select a similar\-looking or similar\-sounding word without checking its meaning\. If the applicable entry or permission is missing or ambiguous\, refer the term and its intended use to the responsible lexical editor\; do not claim lexical conformance\.

Applies to: general

### Exceptions

- This draft pilot supplies no controlled vocabulary\. Its fictional local entries demonstrate review decisions only\; neither an example term nor the surrounding prose establishes global IT\-STE lexical approval\.

- Preserve exact identifiers\, commands\, interface strings\, and quotations in marked literal spans\. Their preservation does not approve their words for editable prose\. Explain a misleading literal in surrounding prose instead of silently renaming it\.

- Do not import ASD dictionary entries\, aviation\-specific meanings\, inflection tables\, or technical\-term categories as IT\-STE vocabulary decisions\. Use the applicable reviewed IT entry\. A technical noun and verb may share a spelling when their functions and meanings are independently approved\.

- A lexical or style rewrite must not change technical meaning\, actors\, conditions\, modality\, or safety and security constraints\. If an external requirement conflicts with these constraints\, stop the rewrite and escalate it to the responsible editor or technical owner\.

- Supporting target IDs identify related planned work\, including targets not materialized in this pilot\. They neither delegate these source facets nor establish canonical relation endpoints\.

It permits refresh\, refreshes\, refreshed\, and refreshing only for that status\-panel operation\, not credential rotation\. Example instruction\: Refresh the current service status\.

Rationale: The instruction uses the base verb with the meaning and context documented by this fictional local entry\. This is not an actual vocabulary entry or global approval of refresh\.

## ITSTE-R-0003

### Keep authoritative names consistent within context

Identify the applicable terminology authority for each concept and use its name consistently within the documented product\, version\, and interface layer\. Check the relevant standard\, schema\, product specification\, or maintained project glossary before choosing a name\. Do not alternate synonyms for the same concept for stylistic variety\. If different layers or versions expose different names for the same concept\, state their mapping and scope explicitly at first relevant use\. Preserve each external name exactly when referring to that interface\. Distinguish genuinely different concepts instead of forcing them under one convenient name\. When authorities disagree within the same scope\, ask the responsible terminology or technical owner to resolve the conflict before claiming consistency\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, configuration, source\-code

### Exceptions

- A documented contextual short form may follow the full name when its referent remains unique\. It is an explicit alias\, not permission to introduce arbitrary synonyms\.

- Different interface layers\, product versions\, and separately defined concepts can legitimately have different names\. A mapping must not imply equivalence unless the technical owner confirms it\.

- Do not rename commands\, fields\, proper names\, quoted text\, or interface strings to match prose terminology\. Preserve literal spelling and explain the relationship outside the literal\.

### Positive examples

In this guide\, the project glossary calls the queued work item a job\. The scheduler assigns a job to a worker\. The worker reports the job result\.

Rationale: The same glossary concept keeps one name across its lifecycle\; the local example does not approve job as a global vocabulary entry\.

In this fictional product\, the interface label `Workspace` corresponds to the API field `tenant_id`\. Use the label in interface instructions and the field name in API requests\.

Rationale: The explicit mapping distinguishes two naming layers while preserving their different external spellings\.

### Negative examples

The glossary names the object a job\. Submit the job\, inspect the task\, and archive the request\; all three names refer to that same object\.

Rationale: Unmapped synonyms make one object look like three concepts\. Keep job in this stated glossary context\.

The documented API field is `tenant_id`\, but call the field `workspace_id` in requests so that it matches the interface terminology\.

Rationale: The proposed rename changes the interface contract\. An explanatory mapping must preserve the real field name\, not replace it\.

## ITSTE-R-0004

### Choose terms that international readers can understand

When no applicable authority fixes a term\, choose the shortest term that identifies the concept unambiguously for the intended international audience\. Compare candidates for meaning and reader familiarity\, not only length\. Avoid regional expressions\, slang\, and unexplained internal nicknames\. Keep a longer term when shortening would remove a necessary distinction\. Use established IT terminology when it is familiar to the intended audience\; otherwise define it at first relevant use with the concept and its distinguishing property\. Make the definition available in independently read sections that need it\. Submit a newly selected term through terminology review rather than treat its use in a draft as admission\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, user\-interface, error\-messages, support\-troubleshooting

### Exceptions

- An authoritative name takes precedence over a locally preferred shorter name\. Preserve exact external labels and names\, and explain unfamiliar wording instead of silently renaming them\.

- Established or clearly defined specialist IT terms are not prohibited merely because a general audience might call them jargon\. Judge familiarity against the actual audience and supply necessary definitions\.

- There is no three\-word ceiling\. A shorter ambiguous term is not preferable to a longer accurate term\; a local abbreviation also needs a clear introduction\.

### Positive examples

For this fictional support guide\, no authority fixes the term\. Call the location for rejected messages a failed\-message queue\, and define it as the queue that holds messages after delivery attempts fail\.

Rationale: The selected term identifies a concrete role without an internal nickname\. The definition limits its meaning\; this is a local selection example\, not lexical approval\.

A tombstone is a stored marker that records a deletion so that replicas can apply that deletion\. Keep the marker until every required replica has received it\.

Rationale: The established specialist term is explained rather than prohibited or replaced with an inaccurate shorter word\. The retention condition remains explicit\.

### Negative examples

Put the undeliverable message in the naughty corner\.

Rationale: The metaphor is an unexplained local nickname\, not a clear international name for a message location\.

Rename the failed\-message queue to queue everywhere because one word is always clearer\.

Rationale: The shorter name loses the distinction between failed messages and normal work\. Brevity does not justify ambiguity\.

## ITSTE-R-0005

### Use American spelling without changing external text

Use American English spelling consistently in editable canonical English prose\. Check uncertain spellings against the applicable American English reference and project terminology authority\. If a governing contract\, specification\, style guide\, or other mandatory external directive requires another variety\, identify its scope and apply that variety consistently within that scope\. Do not use spelling normalization to change a quotation\, proper name\, localized example\, or externally controlled or machine\-significant string\. Keep those spans exact and apply the prose baseline only outside them\. If directives conflict or their scope is unclear\, refer the issue to the responsible editor before rewriting\.

Applies to: general

### Exceptions

- This rule controls spelling\, not vocabulary admission\, product naming\, or a wholesale change of grammar or terminology\. It does not import the ASD dictionary as the spelling or lexical authority\.

- A required localized example can demonstrate another variety\. Identify the locale and preserve the example rather than present it as an accidental canonical prose spelling\.

- Preserved paths\, identifiers\, commands\, interface labels\, and quotations may contain non\-American spellings\. Their words gain no general prose approval from preservation\.

### Positive examples

Check the color of the status indicator\.

Rationale: Color follows the American spelling baseline in editable prose when no contrary directive applies\.

The configuration key is `colour`\. Use this key to set the indicator color\.

Rationale: The externally defined key keeps its exact spelling while the surrounding prose uses color\.

### Negative examples

Check the colour of the status indicator\.

Rationale: With no governing exception or quotation in this example\, editable canonical prose uses the American spelling color\.

The schema defines `colour`\, but send `color` as the key to conform to American spelling\.

Rationale: Normalizing a machine\-significant key changes the schema contract\. The prose baseline never authorizes this edit\.

## ITSTE-R-0006

### Expose the structure of multi\-word terms

Make the head noun and modifier relationships of a multi\-word expression clear\. For editable descriptive wording\, replace an ambiguous stack with explicit grammatical relationships\, such as a phrase that identifies what belongs to what or which operation affects which object\. Preserve an authoritative term rather than shorten or respell it\. Write a long term in full at first use in each independently consumable unit\; introduce a contextual short form or abbreviation only when it improves readability and has one clear referent\. Preserve established hyphenation\. Add hyphens only when standard spelling or grammar requires them to connect related words\, not to reduce a count\. Distinguish a hyphen from a dash\, and preserve syntax\-controlled characters exactly\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, error\-messages, logs\-events, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, source\-code

### Exceptions

- There is no absolute three\-word limit\. A long authoritative term can remain intact\; explain its role outside the name if its internal wording is difficult\.

- An alias introduced in another page\, help topic\, or retrieved fragment is not sufficient when the current unit can be read alone\. Reintroduce it where needed\.

- Do not add hyphens inside names or identifiers\, substitute typographic dashes for command hyphens\, or join unrelated words to satisfy a local length policy\. A preserved literal need not follow prose hyphenation\.

### Positive examples

Check the retention policy for logs from the backup agent\.

Rationale: The phrase states that the logs come from the agent and that the policy concerns those logs\, instead of stacking four modifiers\.

In this self\-contained fictional help topic\, open `Regional Access Policy Manager` \(the policy manager\)\. The policy manager lists the regional access policies\. The illustrative option `--read-only` retains its two leading hyphens\.

Rationale: The full interface name precedes a unique contextual alias\. The option is an exact syntax illustration\, not an instruction to execute a command\.

### Negative examples

Check the backup agent log retention policy\.

Rationale: In the intended relationship shown by the positive example\, the modifier stack makes the policy scope harder to identify\. State that it is the retention policy for logs from the backup agent\.

Rewrite the illustrative option `--read-only` as `—read-only` and add hyphens throughout the interface name to reduce its word count\.

Rationale: A typographic dash is not two syntax hyphens\, and counting does not justify altering a name\. Negative literals record the proposed error\, not valid alternate syntax\.

## ITSTE-R-0007

### Simplify verbs without losing time or state

Choose the simplest verb construction that preserves the technical meaning\. Prefer simple tenses and explicit clauses when they express the same facts\. Retain perfect or progressive aspect when it distinguishes prior completion\, present result\, duration\, or activity in progress\. Remove redundant auxiliary wording\, but keep auxiliaries that carry required time\, uncertainty\, permission\, or obligation\. Make a participial state distinguishable from an action\: add the relevant time\, actor\, or explicit state wording when the distinction matters\. Permit established nouns and clear modifiers ending in ing\; rewrite dense chains so that the operation\, object\, and relationship are explicit\. Use forms permitted by the applicable IT lexical entry rather than infer permission from spelling alone\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, user\-interface, error\-messages, logs\-events, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, support\-troubleshooting

### Exceptions

- This rule does not impose the ASD tense whitelist\, its prohibition on perfect constructions\, or its restriction of ing forms to technical nouns and modifiers\. Necessary aspect and modality remain available\.

- A participle after be does not by itself prove either a passive action or a state\. Determine the intended technical claim before rewriting\; do not invent completion from a report of ongoing activity\.

- Exact status strings\, identifiers\, logs\, and quotations remain literal\. Clarify their timing or meaning in surrounding prose instead of editing them\.

### Positive examples

The worker is copying the snapshot now\. The copy has not finished\.

Rationale: Progressive aspect expresses current activity\; simplifying to a completed event would change the claim\.

The volume remains encrypted while the service is stopped\. The key service encrypted the volume before the shutdown\.

Rationale: The first sentence describes a continuing state\; the second identifies a completed action and its time relationship\. Neither claim depends on treating every participle as passive\.

### Negative examples

The agent is in the process of performing a check of the queue now\.

Rationale: Write The agent is checking the queue now\. This removes the redundant process and support\-verb wording while retaining progressive aspect and the explicit current time\; it does not turn ongoing work into a completed event\.

The ongoing routing monitoring logging processing is failing\.

Rationale: The dense ing chain hides the failed operation and its object\. If the intended fact is that the logger cannot process events from the routing monitor\, state that fact explicitly instead of guessing from the stack\.

## ITSTE-R-0008

### Name relevant actors without inventing responsibility

Use active voice when the actor or responsible component matters to understanding or performing the action\. Name the service\, role\, team\, or automated agent that performs it\; use a direct imperative for an action assigned to the reader\. Before changing passive wording\, verify who actually acts and whether the action is automatic or manual\. Use passive voice when the actor is unknown\, irrelevant\, intentionally omitted for security\, or less important than the affected object or state\. Keep any responsibility\, timing\, condition\, and required response explicit even when passive voice is justified\. If responsibility is necessary but unknown\, report or escalate that gap instead of guessing an actor\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, user\-interface, error\-messages, logs\-events, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, security, support\-troubleshooting

### Exceptions

- Object\-focused incident descriptions and state reports can validly use passive voice\. Do not invent a component or expose a protected identity merely to force an active sentence\.

- A justified passive description must not replace a required reader instruction with a vague capability statement\. Assign the reader action or identify the responsible non\-reader actor\.

- Preserve exact external messages and quotations\. A state adjective is not automatically passive voice\; review the intended claim\, not just a form of be\.

### Positive examples

The deployment controller restarts the worker after the health check fails\.

Rationale: The verified component and triggering condition make an automatic action explicit\; it is not assigned to the reader\.

The audit record was deleted during the outage\. The investigation has not identified who deleted it\.

Rationale: The actor is explicitly unknown\. Passive voice reports the observed event without inventing responsibility\.

### Negative examples

Routine reader instruction\: The deployment log can be opened\.

Rationale: When the intended action belongs to the reader\, write Open the deployment log\. Passive capability leaves the instruction unassigned\.

The administrator deleted the audit record\. Editorial context\: the investigation has established the deletion but has not identified who performed it\.

Rationale: Adding an unsupported administrator falsely attributes responsibility\. Preserve the known event and explicitly report the unknown actor instead\.

## ITSTE-R-0009

### Express operations with direct verbs

When editable prose describes an action\, use a direct verb permitted for that meaning and context if it is clearer than a nominalization or a support\-verb phrase\. Replace wording such as perform a validation with the corresponding direct action only when the actor\, object\, timing\, modality\, and effect remain the same\. Rebuild the sentence as needed rather than mechanically exchange words\. Keep a noun when it names a concept\, state\, result\, interface element\, or machine\-significant entity instead of hiding an action\. If no applicable verb entry exists\, request lexical review\; do not turn an admitted noun into a verb solely to shorten the sentence\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, error\-messages, logs\-events, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, source\-code, support\-troubleshooting

### Exceptions

- A concept such as authentication or a result such as a validation report can be the actual subject of discussion\. Do not force every action\-related noun into a verb\.

Rationale: In a context where validate is admitted for this check\, the direct verb replaces Perform a validation of the configuration without changing the prerequisite\.

Select `Validation` to view the validation report\.

Rationale: The surrounding action is direct\, while the interface label and report concept remain nouns\. The label is illustrative\, not an actual product specification\.

### Negative examples

Perform the execution of a validation of the configuration before the deployment starts\.

Rationale: In the same intended context as the positive example\, the support\-verb chain hides the operation\. Use the direct validation verb and keep the timing condition\.

Rename the tab `Validation` to `Validate` because action\-related nouns are prohibited\.

## ITSTE-R-0010

## ITSTE-R-0011

### Retain the words that make prose unambiguous

In editable full\-sentence prose\, include the words needed for grammatical completeness and for clear actors\, actions\, objects\, conditions\, and references\. Use articles and demonstratives where standard English and the intended specificity require them\; distinguish a new item from an identified item without inserting a determiner mechanically\. Retain the conjunction that when it clarifies the boundary of a subordinate clause or helps international comprehension\. Spell out contractions in authored prose\. Do not delete necessary words merely to reduce length\. Keep the understood reader subject in an imperative\, and supply an object only when the verb and intended meaning require one\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, error\-messages, logs\-events, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, release\-notes, source\-code, support\-troubleshooting, ai\-automation

### Exceptions

- Headings\, labels\, table cells\, diagrams\, logs\, CLI syntax\, source code\, and other constrained content can use conventional fragments when their context supplies the omitted grammar and the meaning remains clear\. Do not use that exception to make an ordinary procedural sentence ambiguous\.

- Standard English permits bare plurals\, mass nouns\, and proper names in suitable contexts\. This rule does not require an article before every noun or a blanket article ban around an identifier\; review the complete construction and intended specificity\.

- Preserve quotations\, externally controlled strings\, and machine\-significant text exactly\, including contractions or omitted words\. Clarify them in surrounding prose rather than expanding inside the literal\.

- The source recommendation about that is guidance\, not a mandate to insert that after every verb\. Use it where it improves the clause boundary without changing meaning\.

### Positive examples

Confirm that the worker has received the configuration\. Then wait\.

Rationale: That marks the subordinate clause\, articles identify the relevant items\, and wait needs neither an expressed reader subject nor an invented object\.

Do not retry the request\. The fictional interface displays `Can't connect`\. In the status table\, the heading Connection status can introduce the fragment Unavailable\.

Rationale: The authored prohibition spells out the contraction\, the external label remains exact\, and a clearly identified table context permits a conventional fragment\.

### Negative examples

Confirm worker received configuration\, then wait it\.

Rationale: In this full\-sentence instruction\, missing determiners blur the intended items\; wait it also adds an object that the intended verb construction does not permit\. Use the positive wording\.

## ITSTE-R-0012

### Make lists parallel and easy to scan

Use a list when separate items make alternatives\, prerequisites\, components\, or actions easier to find than a continuous sentence\. Introduce the list with a lead\-in that states its purpose and\, where needed\, whether all items or only selected alternatives apply\. Make each item connect grammatically to the lead\-in\. Use parallel constructions for items with the same function\, and keep punctuation and item style consistent\. Separate items that have different logical levels or functions instead of mixing descriptions\, prerequisites\, and commands in one undifferentiated list\. Use an ordered list when sequence or priority matters\; otherwise\, use an unordered list\. Preserve the actual execution order and branch conditions\. Follow the governing output format or style guide for presentation details\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, error\-messages, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, release\-notes, support\-troubleshooting

### Exceptions

- A short sentence or a table can be clearer than a list\. Choose the structure that exposes the relationships\; do not convert every series into a vertical list\.

- A constrained interface or machine\-readable format can require its own separators\, ordering\, and item syntax\. Preserve exact commands\, identifiers\, interface strings\, and other literals\; apply prose parallelism only to editable wording\.

- A list item is not automatically one executable step\. Keep technically coupled actions\, required results\, and verification together when appropriate\, without using parallel presentation to hide independent actions or required protection\.

Rationale: The ordered items are parallel reader actions whose sequence matters\. The referent of its is the import selected in the preceding step\.

## ITSTE-R-0013

Use a pronoun or demonstrative only when its intended referent is clear within the unit that readers can receive independently\. If competing referents remain\, name the actual person\, role\, object\, action\, state\, or condition\. After a prohibition or in a risk explanation\, identify whether a consequence follows from the prohibited action\, the protective action\, or the stated condition\. If the source does not establish the relationship or referent\, obtain clarification instead of guessing\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, user\-interface, error\-messages, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, release\-notes, support\-troubleshooting, ai\-automation

## ITSTE-R-0014

### Separate independently executable actions into steps

Put each independently executable action in a separate procedural step so that the reader or automation can follow\, verify\, resume\, and report it separately\. Keep multiple actions in one step only when a technical dependency makes them simultaneous\, atomic\, conventionally inseparable\, or immediately coupled to a required result or verification\. Preserve intentional transaction boundaries\. Prefer one principal instruction per sentence\; permission to group coupled actions in a step does not justify a dense sentence of independent instructions\. A step can contain multiple sentences that state coupled actions\, expected results\, limits\, or verification\. Keep the conditions and required results with the action they govern\. Describe automatic system behavior as behavior of the named system rather than invent a reader action\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, support\-troubleshooting, ai\-automation

### Exceptions

- Do not split a command\, expression\, code example\, or atomic transaction into artificial actions\. Preserve exact literal syntax\, and explain its execution boundary in surrounding prose when necessary\.

- A result or immediately required verification can remain in the action\'s step\. Use separate sentences for clarity\; do not separate the verification so far that the reader can continue before checking it\.

- Technical coupling\, not formatting convenience or a desired step count\, justifies grouping\. A conditional branch can contain several separate steps while keeping its controlling condition explicit\.

- Preserve actors\, timing\, modality\, branch conditions\, limits\, and protection\. If a governing requirement conflicts with a safe\, meaning\-preserving division\, stop the rewrite and consult the responsible editor or technical owner\.

- Examples are editorial scenarios\, not complete operational procedures or vocabulary approvals\. Supporting IDs name related planned targets\, not canonical endpoints or delegated source facets\.

### Positive examples

```
To view the completed import report:
1. Open the import history.
2. Select the completed import.
3. Open its report.
```

Rationale: Each action is independently executable and has its own step\. A reader can identify the last completed action without interpreting a chain inside one step\.

1\. Save the notification preferences\. The settings service displays a confirmation\. Verify that the confirmation lists the intended recipients before you continue\.

Rationale: The save action\, immediate system result\, and required verification form one coupled unit\. Separate sentences preserve the sentence\-level preference\, and the service remains the actor that displays the result\.

### Negative examples

1\. Open the import history\, select the completed import\, and open its report\.

Rationale: Three independently executable actions are compressed into one sentence and step without a technical coupling reason\. Separate them as in the positive example\.

```
1. Save the notification preferences.
2. Continue to the next setup task.
3. Verify that the save confirmation lists the intended recipients.
```

Rationale: The required verification is separated from the save and placed after continuation\. Splitting has broken the coupled acceptance boundary instead of improving resumability\.

## ITSTE-R-0015

### Address reader actions with a direct imperative

When an instruction assigns an action to the reader\, express that action with a direct imperative verb and leave the reader as its understood subject\. Put any prerequisite or branch condition before the action that depends on it\; the imperative verb need not be the first word of the sentence\. Do not replace a reader instruction with a passive statement of capability\, permission\, or future activity\. If another actor performs the action\, name that actor and describe its action explicitly instead of implying that the reader performs it\. Distinguish an instruction from a system description\, a permission\, and a policy obligation\. Do not add must to an imperative merely to make it sound stronger\. Preserve the required actor\, timing\, modality\, and safety or security constraints when you rewrite\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, support\-troubleshooting, ai\-automation

### Exceptions

- A description of system behavior or work assigned to another role is not a reader instruction\. Name the system\, service\, role\, team\, or automated agent that performs the action\; do not turn an automatic action into a manual step\.

- Retain the modality required for genuine obligations\, permissions\, and safety or security constraints by their governing content type\. This rule does not prohibit must in those contexts or authorize turning a permission into an instruction\.

- Preserve commands\, identifiers\, quotations\, and interface strings exactly in literal spans\. Apply this rule to the surrounding editable instruction\, not to external syntax\.

- If a rewrite conflicts with a governing external requirement or would alter technical meaning\, stop and refer the conflict to the responsible editor or technical owner\. Do not remove a prerequisite to make a sentence begin with a verb\.

- Supporting target IDs refer to related planned work\, possibly not materialized in the pilot\. They are not canonical relation endpoints and do not transfer this source allocation\.

### Positive examples

If the deployment is paused\, open the deployment log\.

Rationale: The reader receives a direct imperative after the condition that determines whether to act\. Open is the action verb even though the sentence starts with If\.

After the upload completes\, the import service checks the file format automatically\. You do not start this check\.

Rationale: This is an explicitly named system description\, not a reader action\. An imperative would incorrectly assign the automatic check to the reader\.

### Negative examples

If the deployment is paused\, the deployment log can be opened\.

Rationale: When the intended requirement is for the reader to open the log\, a passive capability statement does not assign the action\. Use the condition\-led imperative in the positive example\; do not perform this rewrite if the source actually means permission or capability\.

If the deployment is paused\, you must open the deployment log\.

Rationale: In this illustrative routine instruction\, must was added only for emphasis\. Use the direct imperative\. This negative judgment does not apply to a genuine policy obligation or a required safety constraint\.

## ITSTE-R-0016

### Put conditions before the actions they control

State a prerequisite or condition before the action that depends on it\. Make clear which action or sequence the condition controls and whether multiple conditions all apply or are alternatives\. For a short condition clause\, separate the condition from the instruction with clear punctuation\, normally a comma\. For complex conditions\, use separate sentences\, a list\, or an explicit control\-flow structure before the dependent steps\. Give an explicit stop or escalation path when an unmet prerequisite prevents continuation\. Do not make readers begin work before they discover that it does not apply or cannot proceed\. Keep the controlling condition available with an independently retrieved action\. Express reader actions as imperatives without requiring the action verb to be the sentence\'s first word\; name any different actor truthfully\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, error\-messages, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, support\-troubleshooting, ai\-automation

### Exceptions

- A result that becomes observable only after an action can follow it\. Distinguish such a result or subsequent decision from a prerequisite that must be evaluated before acting\.

- A shared condition can precede a clearly bounded group of steps rather than be repeated mechanically\. If a step is delivered independently\, include the condition or a reliable context mechanism that supplies it before the action\.

- Preserve exact commands\, expressions\, interface strings\, and other literal syntax\. Explain the condition before the literal rather than rearrange executable syntax to imitate English word order\.

- Do not invent a condition\, actor\, permission\, or failure response\. Resolve missing technical meaning with the responsible owner\. Stop and escalate any governing\-requirement conflict that would remove necessary conditions\, change modality or limits\, or weaken protection\.

- Examples illustrate condition placement\, not complete runbooks or vocabulary approval\. Supporting target IDs are planning references\, not canonical endpoints or delegated source allocations\.

### Positive examples

If the import status is complete\, open the import report\. If the status is still running\, wait for completion before opening the report\.

Rationale: The reader evaluates each status condition before its dependent action\. The imperative follows the condition rather than forcing an unconditional verb\-first instruction\.

```
Before you continue, verify both prerequisites:
- The maintenance owner has approved the service window.
- The deployment status is paused.
If either prerequisite is not satisfied, stop and contact the maintenance owner. If both are satisfied, open the deployment log.
```

Rationale: The list states a conjunction\, gives its scope and stop path\, and places all prerequisites before the dependent action\.

### Negative examples

Open the import report\, but only if the import status is complete\.

Rationale: The controlling condition arrives after the command\. Put the status test first so the reader does not act before deciding whether the instruction applies\.

Open the deployment log\. The maintenance owner must have approved the service window\, and the deployment must be paused\; otherwise\, stop\.

Rationale: The prerequisites and stop response follow the dependent action\. Their presence does not repair the ordering\; state and evaluate them first\.

## ITSTE-R-0017

### Reserve notes for supplemental information

Use a note only for supplemental information that is not necessary to complete the procedure correctly\. Keep required actions\, prerequisites\, limits\, acceptance criteria\, expected results needed for execution or verification\, and safety\- or security\-critical information in the main procedural flow\. Label protective instructions\, requirements\, and other semantic blocks according to their actual function rather than disguise them as notes\. Place required information before the action that depends on it\, or with the action when it is an immediate result or verification\. Test the procedure with its notes hidden\: the remaining content must still identify what to do\, when to proceed\, what to verify\, and when to stop\. Preserve that completeness in independently retrieved procedure fragments\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, cli, user\-interface, error\-messages, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, support\-troubleshooting, ai\-automation

### Exceptions

- A note can explain optional background or a nonessential historical fact\. An expected result may be supplemental only when it is not needed to execute\, verify\, choose a branch\, or proceed correctly\.

- A required warning or other semantic block may be visually distinct from numbered steps\, but it remains part of the mandatory flow and must not be hidden or treated as optional\. Follow governing labels and accessibility requirements\.

- A necessary inline identifier in parentheses is not automatically a skippable note\. Preserve exact identifiers\, commands\, quotations\, and interface strings\; retain required identification where the reader needs it\.

- A remote note or reference may supplement but cannot replace local prerequisites\, protection\, or decision criteria\. Do not change actors\, modality\, conditions\, limits\, or technical meaning to relocate content\. Stop and escalate governing\-requirement conflicts to the responsible editor or technical owner\.

- Examples are editorial scenarios\, not complete operational procedures or vocabulary approvals\. Supporting IDs are related planned work\, not canonical endpoints or transferred source allocations\.

### Positive examples

```
1. Open the import history.
2. Select the completed import.
3. Open its report.
Note: Earlier product versions called this view the import summary.
```

Rationale: The historical name is supplemental\. Hiding the note does not remove an action\, prerequisite\, or verification criterion from the report\-viewing sequence\.

```
1. Save the notification preferences. The settings service displays a confirmation. Verify that the confirmation lists the intended recipients before you continue.
Note: The confirmation uses the same typography as the settings page.
```

Rationale: The required result and verification remain in the step\, while the note contains only nonessential visual background\. The settings service is the actor displaying the confirmation\.

### Negative examples

```
1. Save the notification preferences.
2. Continue to the next setup task.
Note: Before continuing, verify that the save confirmation lists the intended recipients.
```

Rationale: The required verification is hidden in a note after continuation\. Promote it into the save step before the reader can proceed\.

```
1. Open the deployment log.
Note: Proceed only if the deployment is paused and the maintenance owner has approved the service window.
```

Rationale: The note contains mandatory prerequisites\. Hiding it makes the action unconditional\, and its late position also violates condition\-first ordering\.

## ITSTE-R-0018

### Sequence information around the reader\'s task

Present purpose\, scope\, prerequisites\, and core concepts before details that depend on them\. Move from information the intended reader can recognize to new or specialized information\, and introduce necessary concepts before using them to explain an operation\. Defer only optional detail through progressive disclosure\. Give each independently retrievable section\, help topic\, or generated chunk enough local context to identify its subject\, applicability\, and decision\-relevant references\. Keep required prerequisites\, protective instructions\, and necessary consequences available with the action before the reader can act\. Keep one principal subject in each sentence when possible\. When a sentence needs multiple subjects\, state their relationship explicitly\. Preserve the actual actors and operational sequence instead of imposing an explanatory order that changes system behavior\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, user\-interface, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, networking, data\-storage, cloud\-infrastructure, release\-notes, support\-troubleshooting, ai\-automation

### Exceptions

- An expert reference can rely on documented audience knowledge or a heading and container that accompany the content\. Do not copy unrelated background into every unit\, but do not assume an omitted chunk supplies required context\.

- An urgent protective instruction can precede background explanation\. Progressive disclosure never makes a required condition\, branch choice\, limit\, acceptance criterion\, or protective action optional\.

- A link or expansion can hold optional detail\. Required context may use a delivery mechanism only if it reliably supplies that context before the dependent action\; a skippable link alone is insufficient\.

- Preserve exact commands\, identifiers\, quotations\, interface strings\, and other literal syntax\. The one\-principal\-subject preference is a focus control\, not a ban on comparisons or a universal sentence\-length limit\.

- Do not invent actors\, causal relationships\, permissions\, or prerequisites\. Stop and consult the responsible editor or technical owner if a governing requirement conflicts with preserving technical meaning\, modality\, limits\, or protection\. Examples grant no vocabulary approval\; supporting IDs are planned references\, not canonical endpoints or delegated provenance\.

### Positive examples

```
Import reports identify records rejected by a completed import. To inspect a report:
1. Open the import history.
2. Select the completed import.
3. Open its report.
The field reference provides optional detail about each report column.
```

Rationale: Purpose and the completed\-import context precede the separately executable navigation steps\. Optional field definitions follow the core task rather than obscure it\.

Deployment\-log help for a paused deployment\: Before you continue\, confirm that the maintenance owner has approved the service window\. If approval is missing\, stop and contact the owner\. If approval is confirmed\, open the deployment log\.

Rationale: The independently readable help unit names its subject and status scope\, retains its prerequisite and stop path\, and gives the dependent reader action last\.

### Negative examples

Rationale: Specialized column details precede the report\'s purpose and access context\. Introduce the report and task first\, then offer optional column detail\.

Independently delivered help fragment\: Open its log\. Expand the optional background section to learn which deployment status and maintenance approval are required\.

Rationale: The fragment omits the deployment referent and makes prerequisites optional\. Neither a remote antecedent nor progressive disclosure can supply a condition after the reader acts\.

## ITSTE-R-0019

Use the native structures of the content type rather than force every format into conventional paragraphs\.

Applies to: general, software\-documentation, api, user\-interface, procedures\-runbooks, configuration, security, networking, data\-storage, cloud\-infrastructure, release\-notes, support\-troubleshooting, ai\-automation

## ITSTE-R-0021

## ITSTE-R-0022

### Explain credible consequences without unsafe disclosure

After the protective action or condition\, explain the credible risk or possible result when it is known and can be disclosed\. Identify the affected people\, data\, system\, or service\, and state the consequence specifically enough to support the reader\'s decision\. Name the hazardous action or condition when a pronoun could confuse it with obeying the protection\. Distinguish a possible consequence from a certain outcome\. Do not invent a cause\, probability\, guarantee\, or recovery assurance\. If details are unknown or security\-sensitive\, state what is known at an appropriate level\, retain a usable precaution\, and provide an authorized escalation path for information needed to proceed\. Keep the necessary explanation with the protection in independently delivered content\. An explanation does not replace the protective instruction or justify placing it after the hazardous operation\.

Applies to: general, security, monitoring\-alerts, procedures\-runbooks

### Exceptions

## ITSTE-R-0029

### Make action and ownership relationships explicit

Make the relationship between an action and its modifiers unambiguous\. If with could describe association\, accompaniment\, a means\, or a condition\, state the intended relationship explicitly while retaining the primary action verb\. Put a prerequisite or condition before its dependent action\. Use a grammatically correct possessive when the owner or associated entity and the relationship are clear\. Rewrite confusing possessive chains with explicit noun phrases or an of construction\, and check that the rewrite preserves the same ownership or association\. If the intended relationship is unclear\, obtain clarification from the authoritative source or technical owner rather than choose an unsupported interpretation\.

Applies to: general

### Exceptions

- Neither with nor possessives nor apostrophes are universally prohibited\. A clear means phrase can retain with\; a clear possessive can be simpler than an elaborate replacement\.

- Possession can express association\, not only legal ownership\. Do not invent an owner or reverse a relationship while expanding a phrase\.

- Preserve externally controlled names\, quotations\, code\, paths\, and other machine\-significant literals exactly\. Apply prose rewrites outside those spans\, not inside identifiers or personal names\.

Rationale: The explicit tool context makes with unambiguous\, and Check remains the primary action verb\. There is no need to ban the preposition or replace the instruction with a vague Use construction\.

Editorial context\: a service has one assigned owner\, and that owner has one escalation contact\. Description\: The service owner\'s escalation contact reviews the report\.

Rationale: The possessive is grammatical and the stated context resolves the association\. Possessive wording is permitted when clear\.

### Negative examples

Check the connection with the proxy enabled\.

Rationale: Without context\, the modifier can identify a connection by its proxy setting or require enabling the proxy during the check\. Ask which relationship is intended\. For the latter meaning\, use the condition\-first instruction\: While the proxy is enabled\, check the connection\.

Rationale: The chain does not establish whether client is software or a customer\, or which owner\'s settings are intended\. Obtain the relationships\, then name the relevant entity explicitly rather than guess an of\-chain replacement\.

### D-P154-006

Term: acrid; POS: adj.

Status: draft; editorial\-review\-needed; candidate disposition: not\-applicable.

Domains: general, procedures\-runbooks

If a power supply emits smoke, stop the maintenance task and follow the site emergency procedure.

The acrid log output means that the service is dangerous.
